# Rhyming 5×5 Chinese poem experiment

## Brief

Compose one 25-character square in simplified Chinese that yields exactly the two requested readings:

- Read each row from left to right, taking rows from top to bottom.
- Read each column from top to bottom, taking columns from left to right.

Each reading must form a coherent five-line poem, with five characters per line. All five endings must rhyme within each poem. Backwards, diagonal, and arbitrary-path readings are not required. The two readings should meaningfully differ.

Rhyme is judged using contemporary standard Mandarin rhyme families; tones are not constrained. This is not a claim to classical regulated-verse meter.

## Composition and review

Three writers received different standalone briefs: a boundary-first physical scene, a formal scaffold, and an everyday human situation. A fourth agent reviewed the resulting candidates.

All three writers and the reviewer were created with no inherited conversation. The writers received none of the earlier screenshots, poems, or other writers’ drafts. The reviewer received only the new candidates and their constraints.

The parent checked character counts, exact transposes, distinct-line counts, and each ending against its contextually checked Mandarin pronunciation. Whole-poem coherence received a separate critical review.

## Recommended: 《归舟》

### The grid / horizontal poem

```text
归 帆 过 渚 晚
舟 隐 村 外 烟
客 路 水 云 漫
梦 随 云 渐 远
寒 山 连 远 天
```

### Vertical poem

```text
归舟客梦寒
帆隐路随山
过村水云连
渚外云渐远
晚烟漫远天
```

### Rhyme audit

Both readings use **《中华通韵》十一安**, which groups **an / ian / uan / üan**. These are one established rhyme family, not four identical phonetic finals. The grouping appears in the [Ministry of Education’s published rhyme table](https://www.moe.gov.cn/s78/A18/tongzhi/201805/t20180521_336620.html).

| Line | Horizontal ending | Final | Vertical ending | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 晚 wǎn | uan | 寒 hán | an |
| 2 | 烟 yān | ian | 山 shān | an |
| 3 | 漫 màn | an | 连 lián | ian |
| 4 | 远 yuǎn | üan | 远 yuǎn | üan |
| 5 | 天 tiān | ian | 天 tiān | ian |

All ten complete lines are different. No terminal character repeats within either individual reading.

### How the poems move

**Across:** The returning sail passes an islet late; the boat disappears into haze beyond a village. The traveler’s route opens into water and clouds; the dream follows the clouds into distance. Cold mountains meet the distant sky.

**Down:** The traveler’s cold dream aboard the returning boat establishes the emotional frame first. The poem then opens outward through the disappearing sail, mountain-following route, village, islet, clouds, and evening haze.

These are different lyric progressions through a shared scene, not unrelated stories.

### Critical assessment

The horizontal poem is the most fluent of the three experiments. The change from outward observation to the drifting dream gives it movement beyond a list of landscape nouns.

The vertical poem also holds together, but **帆隐路随山** is its most compressed line: read **帆隐／路随山**, “the sail disappears; the route follows the mountains.” It does not explicitly describe a boat rounding a bend. **客路水云漫** provides atmosphere more than a sharply defined observation.

Both the parent and the independent reviewer preferred this pair.

## Alternate 1: Formal scaffold

### Grid / horizontal poem

```text
孤舟向暮山
舟中风雨寒
向风望归雁
暮雨归家难
滩寒帆晚还
```

### Vertical poem

```text
孤舟向暮滩
舟中风雨寒
向风望归帆
暮雨归家晚
山寒雁难还
```

### Check and assessment

| Direction | Five endings |
|---|---|
| Across | 山 shān · 寒 hán · 雁 yàn · 难 nán · 还 huán |
| Down | 滩 tān · 寒 hán · 帆 fān · 晚 wǎn · 还 huán |

All endings belong to the same an / ian / uan / üan family. Here **还** means “return,” pronounced **huán**, and **难** means “difficult,” pronounced **nán**.

The transpose is exact. There are nine distinct complete lines; **舟中风雨寒** occurs in both readings.

Across, the observer watches returning geese while human homecoming is difficult and boats return late. Down, the observer watches returning sails, human homecoming is late, and the final difficulty belongs to the geese. Difficulty followed by a late return is not a contradiction.

This has a real directional change, but the inner 4×4 square is symmetric and the readings retain very similar wording and syntax. **暮滩** and **滩寒／帆晚还** also feel somewhat constructed. It is a successful, less distinct alternative to the recommended pair.

## Alternate 2: Old friends and an old building

### Grid / horizontal poem

```text
相对都念旧
看图说旧友
笑问楼谁守
如故不嫌旧
旧友修旧楼
```

### Vertical poem

```text
相看笑如旧
对图问故友
都说楼不修
念旧谁嫌旧
旧友守旧楼
```

### Check and assessment

| Direction | Five endings |
|---|---|
| Across | 旧 jiù · 友 yǒu · 守 shǒu · 旧 jiù · 楼 lóu |
| Down | 旧 jiù · 友 yǒu · 修 xiū · 旧 jiù · 楼 lóu |

These endings rhyme under **ou / iou**, with iou written **iu** after a consonant. This is the 油求 rhyme family, also grouped as **《中华通韵》十欧** in the [same published table](https://www.moe.gov.cn/s78/A18/tongzhi/201805/t20180521_336620.html).

The transpose is exact, all ten complete lines differ, and seven of the ten mirrored off-diagonal character pairs differ. The ending **旧** repeats within both readings; repetition still satisfies the stated rhyme constraint, but is an aesthetic weakness.

Across, friends reminisce over an image, ask who looks after a building, and an old friend repairs it. Down, familiar smiles and an inquiry lead to the statement that the building is not—or will not be—repaired, and an old friend keeps watch over it.

There is a genuine factual difference: **修** across versus **不修** down, followed by **守** rather than repair. However, **如故不嫌旧** has an indistinct attachment for 如故, and both fourth lines feel less vivid than the concrete lines. Nostalgia is repeatedly asserted through 旧.

The vertical poem must not be “rescued” by claiming the friends oppose demolition, want historically sensitive repairs, or secretly repair the building. Those actions are not in its text. Read literally as attachment to an unrepaired building, it remains intelligible, but its reasoning is thin.

The independent reviewer judged this a legitimate alternative, not an improvement on 《归舟》.

## What the experiment showed

For an all-lines-rhyme square, the **bottom row contains every ending of the vertical poem**, while the **rightmost column contains every ending of the horizontal poem**. Each of those edges must therefore be a coherent line made entirely of rhyme words.

The two readings share the lower-right character, so under a single consistent rhyme classification they necessarily share a rhyme family.

The boundary-first approach handled this well: **寒山连远天** and **晚烟漫远天** are meaningful lines before the interior is filled. The more symmetric approach made grammatical construction easier, but produced closer variations. The human-scene approach achieved a factual change between readings, at the cost of repetition and more strained connective lines.

All three candidates passed the explicit size, transpose, and all-line-rhyme checks. Their differences in quality are literary judgments, not additional formal requirements.
